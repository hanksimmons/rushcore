# 13 — Tunnels, the 3× course and the far horizon

**Status:** Design accepted in principle 2026-09-19 (D-112); nothing here is built. Slices T1–T3, W1–W2, H1–H2 below.
**Authority:** below `04` and `03` where they overlap; this document owns the tunnel, the resident-mesh architecture
and the horizon until each slice folds its delivered form back into `04`, `05`, `06` and `11`.
**Read with:** `04 §5D` (the wall profile and the wall shell, D-109/D-111), `04 §5I` (the vertical grammar), `03 §3`
(the ground follow and the wall ride), `docs/11 §7` (the ladder), `DECISIONS` D-096, D-102, D-108–D-112.

## 0. The brief, and the principles it is built under

The user, 2026-09-19: the see-through tube feels completely unnatural and its presentation was the wrong idea; the
concept stays — **tunnels tucked away in the landscape that the player descends into or pierces through walls**.
Clarified: inside a tunnel the ball rides a **slot with a roof** (floor and walls are ground, the roof a ceiling);
both **portals through walls** and **dives beneath the landscape**; tunnels are **hidden optional lines**; sometimes
the surface continues over a tunnel (over or through), sometimes a tunnel is the **only way into a pocket area** the
surface cannot reach; **the camera flies through the tunnel** with the ball. In the same breath: every course at
least **three times as long**, and a **far horizon** of stylized clouds and distant terrain that gives the impression
of a massive world (Breath of the Wild is the reference), cohesive with the stage's look and cheap to draw.

The user's standing rule for all of it: feature-rich, but **KISS, easy to maintain, absolutely robust, optimal game
feel, consistently fun level design.** Everything below is chosen so that:

- **one construct per feature.** A tunnel is one thing (a roofed trench on an optional line), placed two ways. The
  resident mesh is one rule (a coarse mesh everywhere, a fine window around the ball). The horizon is the stage's own
  height source drawn coarsely, not a second world.
- **reuse before invention.** The trench is the corridor-plus-wall stamp with small parameters (`04 §5D`); the
  tunnel's walls are wall shells (D-111); its roof is one more shell strip; the camera inside is the lid's confined
  framing (D-102) generalised; the tunnel line is an offset line (D-100/D-103) with a negative height; the horizon is
  `Relief()` sampled coarsely. No new subsystem.
- **the frozen kit is untouched.** The movement baseline (D-095) and the wall ride (D-108–D-110) are inputs.
  A tunnel is designed around what the ball already does.
- **every rule has a validator and a harness drive** before the user plays it, and every sample stage keeps its
  golden hash until a generator change moves it on purpose.
- **what we will not build:** streaming beyond one stage, more than two mesh levels, SDF or voxel caves, floors
  that share an XZ except the tunnel's own cap, dynamic time of day, a horizon you can drive to.

## 1. What was removed (D-112)

`TubeBuilder`, `TubeMesh`, `TubeDefinition`, the carried tube profiles, the tube validators, the tube follow and
`Movement › Tube Contact`, `IStructureSurface`, the camera push-out, the tube materials and debug drawing, the
scatter keep-out, `ArchetypeRules.TubeChance`, the "tube" sample and its hash, the harness ride. The wall ride keeps
what the tube taught: contacts in the wall band are ground, the carry conserves speed and cancels the coming dip.

## 2. Tunnels

### 2.1 The construct

A **tunnel line** is an optional line (`RouteLineKind.Tunnel`) built by `OptionalLineBuilder` like a ridge line
(D-100) but with a **negative height** (it goes down, not up), a **narrow corridor** and a **roof**. Its geometry
has three parts, each of which already exists in some form:

1. **The trench** is the heightfield. The line's corridor is stamped with the ordinary corridor-plus-wall machinery
   (`StampedLine`, `WallProfile`), with per-line parameters instead of the archetype's: half-width 10 m, no
   setback, a 6 m fillet, a 78° face, and a lip that rounds into the surrounding ground. Where the trench's walls
   reach the surrounding ground the stamp is exactly the ground; where the tunnel is deep the trench walls run up
   to the roof height and the shell takes over. **The ball's floor is the heightfield everywhere**, as `04 §9`
   requires, so the ground follow, the route speed model's touchdown and the validators all keep reading the one
   height source.
2. **The walls are wall shells** (D-111), the tunnel line's own, generated by the same `ShellStrips` with the line's
   profile parameters. The primary's wall shell has a **hole** where the tunnel's corridor crosses it, by the
   existing ownership rule extended (a station is invalid inside another line's full corridor weight), and the
   primary's stamp already yields to the tunnel's corridor there (`04 §5D`: "a ledge's corridor wins over the
   primary's wall zone").
3. **The roof** is a new shell strip, `TunnelRoof`: per tunnel vertex, an arch from the top of one wall to the top of
   the other (a semicircle of radius equal to the half-width plus the wall height above the floor, sampled every
   10°), then the **cap**: a flat top at the surrounding ground's height across the tunnel's width plus the wall
   band, so the surface continues over the tunnel and is **drivable** (a shaped lid, `04 §5I` item 11). At each
   end the roof strip closes with a **portal face**: a vertical face from the cap down to the arch, with the arch as
   its opening. The roof is collided as the wall shells are (a `ConcavePolygonShape3D` on the structure layer,
   backface collision on) and drawn with the terrain material and the strata tint, darkened inside (§2.6).

The heightfield under the cap is the trench; the cap is a structure above it. That is the one place a stage has
two surfaces over one XZ, and it is the lid-as-floor case D-096 already allows. The world's height query
(`MovementToyWorld.SampleHeight`, D-111) gains the ball's own height as context: under a cap it answers the cap's
top when the ball is above the cap's underside minus a ball, the trench otherwise. `IGroundSurface.Height` gets a
`y` parameter with that meaning and every other caller passes nothing (the default: the stamp or the grid as
today). The camera's ground query passes the lens's height for the same reason, so a lens over a cap never sinks
into the cap's solid. Nothing else in the controller changes: a ball in the tunnel rides the trench floor and walls
under the ground and wall-ride rules; a ball on the cap rides a flat structure the way it rides a lid. The tunnel
line's own wall strips stop at the arch's spring line (the trench's walls above it lie inside the cap's solid and
are never seen or touched); the heightfield keeps the full trench because a single-valued field has no other
choice, and nothing reads it there.

### 2.2 Two placements

**Portal (pierce a wall).** On a walled stage (Canyon Run, the Summit's headwalls, a Sky terrace cliff) the tunnel
line leaves the primary through an S-transition (the offset-line S, 200–300 m), runs *into the wall band* and out
the far side, and either rejoins the primary further along (through a second portal) or opens into a pocket
(§2.3). Length 60–250 m inside the rock. The wall is already 60–120 m high, so the portal face is the wall's own
face with an arched opening; the cap is the wall's top. Depth below the surface is the wall height: no dive needed.

**Dive (beneath the landscape).** On any archetype the tunnel line leaves the primary, descends over a ramp
(cosine, grade ≤ 0.10, so 16–30 m of depth over 200–300 m, well inside the route grade and never a launch at the
cap), runs 200–600 m below the surface, and climbs out the same way, rejoining the primary or opening into a
pocket. The dive needs the surface to be at least `RoofCrown + CapThickness` (12 + 4 = 16 m) above the floor at
the portal, so the portal is placed where the ground **rises**: the builder walks the line's ramp until the
surrounding ground (the stamp *without* the tunnel line, `StageHeightField.SampleWithout(line)`) is 16 m above the
floor and puts the portal face there; before it, the trench is an **open cut** (a slot with no roof, rideable
walls), which is the read cue: the player sees the ground swallow the line. Over the covered section the cap
restores the surface at its original height, so a player on the primary can also drive across the top.

Both placements are the same builder with one flag, and the same shell code.

### 2.3 Pockets

A **pocket** is a region no surface route reaches: a bowl 120–200 m across sunk 25–40 m below the surrounding
ground with the wall profile all round its rim (the stamp of a short, wide line section: half-width 60–100 m,
walls everywhere, no setback), holding a loop of the tunnel line's own corridor and a reward (boost rings; later
pickups and an enemy set-piece). The tunnel line enters through one tunnel and leaves through another (or the
same one, reversed, on a dead-end pocket the player must turn in), then rejoins the primary. From above, the
bowl's rim is a cliff: a player can **fall in** (a setback, the D-096 rule: it costs time and Flow, never a restore)
and the only way out is the exit tunnel. On walled archetypes the pocket is a side valley behind the wall; on open
landscapes it is a sinkhole in a hillside. The pocket validator: every cell of the rim is a wall face at least 20 m
tall (no drivable path in), the exit tunnel's floor profile never stalls from a standing start on the pocket floor
(a player who fell in can leave), and the reward stands on the pocket floor inside the walls.

### 2.4 Numbers (provisional, V-017)

| Quantity | Value | Why |
|---|---:|---|
| Corridor half-width | 10 m | two lanes at the ball's scale; the wall ride is a lane change, not a loop |
| Wall fillet radius | 6 m | a rideable fillet that fits a 20 m slot (`WallProfile` parameterised; 30 m is the canyon's) |
| Wall face angle | 78° | steeper than the canyon's 72°: a tunnel wall is a wall, not a bank |
| Roof crown above the floor | 12 m | a full charge under it is refused by the headroom rule, as under a lid (D-102) |
| Cap thickness above the crown | 4 m | the surface stays drivable over the arch |
| Portal face height | ≥ 16 m | crown plus cap; the wall's own height on a portal |
| Dive ramp grade | ≤ 0.10 | never a launch at the cap, inside the route grade |
| Covered length | 60–250 m (portal), 200–600 m (dive) | the read cue at the far end stays visible; long enough to feel |
| Straight required before a portal | 150 m | the approach must aim the ball at the opening from ≥ 300 m out |
| Bends inside | the bend ladder as on the floor | the floor is ground; the walls are a bonus, never required |
| Per stage (6 km / 18 km) | 1–2 / 3–5 | hidden, not everywhere |
| `TunnelChance` | Canyon 0.7, Highlands 0.5, Dune Sea 0.3, Sky 0.4, Summit 0 until S2 | the open landscapes host dives, the walled ones portals |

The tunnel is a **shortcut or an equal-time line with a reward**, never a slower line: the speed model integrates
its floor as ordinary ground (no carried segments exist any more), and the builder rejects a tunnel whose model
time from fork to rejoin exceeds the primary's over the same span by more than 5%.

### 2.5 Camera: inside, with the ball

The lid's confined framing (D-102, `CameraRig.ConfineUnderLids`) becomes **confine under roofs**: `RoofOver(x, z)`
answers the roof's underside height above a plan position for lids *and* tunnels (for a tunnel, the arch's height
at the lens's lateral offset from the centreline), and the lens stays `LidCameraMargin` (1.5 m) below it, its
chase distance shortened to what the tunnel allows and its lateral position clamped inside the walls by the same
margin. The confinement eases in over the 30 m before a portal and out over the 30 m after, so the lens dips under
the arch as the ball goes in and rises as it comes out; the occlusion probe never sees the shell (structure
layer), so nothing pulls the lens through a wall. The framing bands (D-090) keep the ball in frame. Portal read: the
approach straight keeps the opening in the forward cone from 300 m.

### 2.6 Presentation

Rock, not glass. The shells use the terrain material with the canyon's strata tint on the walls and a darker
roof tint; inside, the vertex colour darkens toward the tunnel's middle (a baked gradient by distance from the
nearer portal: full light at the mouths, 45% at 60 m in), so the interior reads as shade without any lighting
change. Readability at 148 m/s comes from two cues: an **emissive guide strip** along each wall at ball height,
every 25 m (the rib idea kept where it worked, as a lantern band cut into the rock, using the existing glow
material), and the **far portal's daylight**, which is real: the exit is visible down the bore. The portal has a
rim: a 1.5 m rock lip around the arch, so the opening reads as an opening against the wall from far off. Nothing is
animated; nothing depends on the environment.

### 2.7 Validators (all in `StageGenerator`, reported, batch-checked)

- **tunnel clearance:** the roof crown is ≥ 12 m above the floor at every vertex and the corridor ≥ 20 m wide,
- **portal:** a portal face ≥ 16 m high, on a straight, with 150 m of straight before it and no feature, lid, fork
  or other portal within 100 m,
- **cap grade:** the cap's top surface grade ≤ the route grade, so the surface over a tunnel is drivable,
- **profile:** the tunnel's floor profile never stalls and its fork-to-rejoin time is within 5% of the primary's,
- **stamp:** beside the middle of the covered section the ground rises from the floor as the 6 m fillet and the 78°
  face within 1 m (the D-109 probe with the tunnel's numbers),
- **hole:** no wall-shell station of any other line stands inside the tunnel's corridor,
- **pocket:** the rim is a wall ≥ 20 m everywhere; the exit tunnel is climbable from a standing start,
- **no crossing:** a tunnel's corridor never overlaps another line's corridor except at its own fork and rejoin (a
  single-valued heightfield cannot pass one floor under another; tunnels run beside the primary, never under it),
- the existing headroom, drain, acyclic-graph and exit validators keep running unchanged.

### 2.8 Harness (08 §5 additions)

- the batch places tunnels on each archetype and every tunnel passes its validators,
- the **tunnel drive**: on a seed with a tunnel (borrowed if the drive seed has none), the follower drives the
  ball off the primary onto the tunnel line and through: grounded ≥ 95% inside, no impact at either portal, the
  lens under the roof and inside the walls on every tick of the covered length, exit speed within 10% of the model,
- the **cap drive**: the ball driven along the surface over a covered section stays grounded on the cap,
- the **pocket fall**: a ball dropped over a pocket's rim lands on the pocket floor grounded and the follower
  drives it out through the exit tunnel,
- a new sample stage takes the "tube" slot in `docs/10`: **tunnel**, the first canyon seed with a portal through
  a wall (seed pinned when T1 lands; its hash recorded then).

- **T2 — the dive (every archetype).** The open cut, the portal placed where the ground rises, `SampleWithout`,

## 3. The 3× course

### 3.1 Targets

| Quantity | Today | Target |
|---|---:|---:|
| Footprint | 6 000 × 2 000 m | 18 000 × 2 000 m |
| Primary route length | 6.3–7.0 km | 19–21 km |
| Run time at the cap | 46–51 s | 140–155 s |
| Heightfield samples at 4 m | 752 k | 2.25 M |
| Wall-shell triangles (canyon) | ~135 k | ~400 k |
| Time to first frame | 4.6–5.5 s | ≤ 2.0 s |

The footprint's width stays: the route band (±600 m) and the optional band (±800 m) fit tunnels and pockets. The
run's rhythm over 18 km is three **acts** of 6 km with their own feature and line spacing multipliers (open cruise,
technical middle, finale), a three-entry table on `ArchetypeRules`; nothing else about difficulty moves (it stays
parked until after Phase 4). How many stages make a run is Phase 5's (with 3× stages a three-stage run is about
eight minutes at the cap).

### 3.2 What scales for free

Route skeleton, corridor stamp, validators and speed profiles are linear in length: 47 ms today, 150–200 ms at 3×.
`Sample()` over 2.25 M grid points is ~150 ms on one core. The Jolt heightfield collider is one shape; its BVH
build at 2.25 M samples is a few hundred milliseconds. Wall shells build in ~0.3 s. Scatter is a `MultiMesh` and
scales with count. Checkpoints (400 m), boost rings (1 200 m) and optional lines (700 m) keep their spacing, so the
economy per kilometre is unchanged.

### 3.3 What does not: the resident mesh

Today the whole stage is drawn as non-indexed flat-shaded tiles at 4 m: 1.5 M triangles, ~130 MB of vertex data,
and most of the 4.6 s build. At 3× that is 4.5 M triangles and ~380 MB. The rule that replaces it, and the only
rendering architecture this plan adds:

- **one coarse mesh, resident:** the same tile builder at **16 m cells** over the whole stage (140 k samples, 280 k
  triangles, ~24 MB), flat-shaded with the same colours, built with the stage; it is what the player sees beyond
  the fine window and what the horizon meets,
- **one fine window, sliding:** 4 m tiles (128 cells = 512 m square) only for tiles whose centre lies within
  **1.2 km of the ball** in plan (a disc, not an X range: the route wanders ±600 m and the spiral pit doubles
  back, so the window follows the ball, not the axis). Over a 2 km-wide footprint that is at most 20 tiles,
  ~650 k triangles resident. The window is re-evaluated every 100 m of travel with 300 m of hysteresis; tiles
  that fall out are freed, tiles that come in are built on a **worker thread** (the vertex arrays are plain C#
  over `_heights`) in order of route distance ahead of the ball and committed on the main thread (one
  `AddSurfaceFromArrays` per tile, a millisecond). The window around the start pad is complete before the run
  starts. Every tile, fine or coarse, carries a 6 m **skirt** along its edges so a fine/coarse seam never shows a
  crack,
- **coarse tiles under a fine tile are hidden**, and that is the whole level-of-detail scheme. Each coarse tile
  is toggled when the fine tile over it arrives or leaves; no per-frame work.
- **the collider is never windowed:** the `HeightMapShape3D` covers the whole stage at 4 m, as today, so physics,
  the follow and the validators see one surface at all times.

Budget: heights 9 MB, collider ~20 MB, coarse mesh 24 MB, fine window ≤ 60 MB, shells ≤ 40 MB. Draw: ~800 k
triangles on screen at the worst view. The camera's far plane goes to 30 km for the horizon (`Camera › Far Plane`);
depth fog begins at 800 m and ends at 6 km (§4.3).

### 3.4 Harness (08 §5 additions)

- the world builds to its first frame in ≤ 2 s on the harness machine (collider, coarse mesh, first window),
- on every kilometre of the drive the fine window covers every tile within 1.0 km of the ball and no fine tile
  farther than 1.6 km is resident (window bounds asserted from the world's tile list),
- resident triangles stay under 1.2 M on every kilometre,
- the coarse mesh's height under the ball agrees with the collider within one coarse cell's chord,
- the full-length drive runs on **one** archetype per harness run (the archetype selector's); the other
  archetypes drive their first 6 km. Wall time per run stays under 4 minutes.

### 3.5 Slices

- **W1 — the resident mesh on the 6 km stage.** Coarse mesh, fine window, worker-thread tile build, hide rule,
  build-time and window checks. No generator change, no hash moves.
- **W2 — the length.** Footprint and route length ×3, acts, per-stage counts, the far plane and fog; every golden
  hash moves and is re-recorded; regression seeds re-picked; the harness drive policy above.

## 4. The far horizon

### 4.1 Horizon terrain

A **horizon ring** from the footprint's edge to 20 km: a flat-shaded mesh whose heights are the stage's own
`Relief()` (swells plus micro relief, plus the dune wave on a dune sea) sampled at 64 m cells to 4 km, 256 m to
10 km and 1 km beyond, **plus a distance envelope** that rises from 0 at 2 km to 600–900 m at 12 km with the
archetype's own noise, so the stage sits in a bowl of foothills that become mountains. At the footprint's edge the
ring's height equals the stage's edge relief (same function, same seed), so there is no seam; past 2 km the envelope
takes over. Per archetype: red mesas and buttes (Canyon Run, the wall profile's silhouette repeated at scale on the
envelope), green hills to blue ridges (Highlands), a dune sea to a distant range (Dune Sea), peaks through the
cloud band (Sky Terraces), a cirque of higher summits (the Summit). Colour comes from `SampleColor` with the fog
doing atmospheric perspective. ~40 k triangles, one mesh, no collider; it is never reachable (the stage boundary
rule that returns a ball leaving the footprint is Phase 5 recovery and is noted there).

### 4.2 Sky

The `ProceduralSkyMaterial` gradient stays (it is cheap and already tuned). Over it, a **cloud dome**: a hemisphere
mesh of 15 km radius with an unshaded shader of two value-noise layers thresholded into hard-edged stylized cumulus,
two-tone (lit top, shaded base), scrolling slowly, with the sun's direction as the light; plus six to ten **hero
clouds**: camera-facing quads at 6–10 km with a procedural cloud sprite for silhouette. One dome draw with two
noise taps per pixel and a handful of quads; no volumetrics, no time of day. The Sky Terraces cloud band (D-103)
stays as it is.

### 4.3 Fog

Depth fog begins at 800 m and ends at 6 km (from 380 m / 2.4 km), with the sky affect raised so the horizon ring
fades into the gradient; height fog is not added. `World › Fog End` remains the sightline instrument.

### 4.4 Harness (08 §5 additions)

- the horizon ring builds on every archetype; no vertex inside the footprint; its height at the footprint's edge
  matches the stage's edge relief within 5 m at 16 probes; ≤ 60 k triangles,
- the dome and hero clouds exist and draw; frame budget measured with `Performance` monitors at the start pad:
  ≤ 1.5 M triangles on screen, ≤ 120 draw calls from the world.

### 4.5 Slices

- **H1 — the horizon ring** with the per-archetype envelope and palette, the far plane and fog.
- **H2 — the cloud dome and hero clouds**, and the user's eye on the whole.

## 5. Order and gates

**T1 → H1 → W1 → T2 → W2 → H2 → T3.** The portal first because it is the feel the user asked for and it lands on
the current stage; the horizon second because it is cheap and transforms the read; the resident mesh third because
the length cannot ship without it; then the dive, the length, the sky, and pockets last because they need all of
the above. Each slice is one commit with its harness green, its hashes re-recorded when a generator rule moved, its
decision logged (D-113 onward) and its owning doc updated. The user plays after T1 + H1 and after W2.

## 6. Decisions the user still owns

- the tunnel's numbers in §2.4 (width, crown, chances) once the first portal is played,
- whether a dead-end pocket (turn around inside) is wanted or every pocket has an exit tunnel,
- the footprint width at 3× (2 000 m kept here),
- the acts' rhythm table,
- the horizon's palette per archetype on sight.
